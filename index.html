About the Project

Antibiotic Literacy Bangladesh was founded in 2025 by a group of passionate microbiology students at BRAC University who recognized the growing threat of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) in our communities.

As future healthcare professionals and scientists, we felt a responsibility to address the knowledge gap surrounding proper antibiotic use. Our initiative aims to bridge this gap through education, awareness campaigns, and community engagement.

Aligned with UN Sustainable Development Goal 3: Good Health and Well-being

Our team consists of undergraduate and graduate students from microbiology, public health, and related disciplines, all united by a common goal: to combat antibiotic resistance through literacy and responsible practices.

Why Antibiotic Resistance Matters

What is Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)?

Antimicrobial resistance occurs when bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites change over time and no longer respond to medicines, making infections harder to treat and increasing the risk of disease spread, severe illness, and death.

Why is AMR a Problem in Bangladesh?

Bangladesh faces significant challenges with antibiotic resistance due to factors such as over-the-counter availability of antibiotics, incomplete treatment courses, and lack of public awareness about proper antibiotic use. This has led to increased treatment failures for common infections.

The Global Impact

AMR is a global health threat that affects everyone, regardless of age or country. It can lead to longer hospital stays, higher medical costs, and increased mortality. Without effective antibiotics, even minor surgeries and routine medical procedures could become high-risk.

Our Role in Combating AMR

Through education and awareness, we can slow the development and spread of antibiotic resistance. Simple actions like completing prescribed antibiotic courses, not sharing antibiotics, and preventing infections through good hygiene can make a significant difference.

Educational Workshops

We conduct workshops in universities, colleges, and community centers to educate students and the public about antibiotic resistance and proper antibiotic use.

Antibiotic Awareness Week

Each year, we organize events during World Antibiotic Awareness Week (November 18-24) to highlight the importance of this global issue.

Student Engagement

We recruit and train student ambassadors from various disciplines to spread awareness about antibiotic resistance in their communities.
